# Statistical Findings

## Constraints for all future hypotheses
1. Strength must be the primary determinant of base win rate
2. Favorites must provide a multiplicative speed boost (~8-10% per fav)
3. Allergies must reduce win rate via a weight-dependent mechanism
4. Heavier pirates must be more resistant to allergy effects
5. Orvinn's apparent allergy immunity is inconclusive — his low strength (52) means
   he needs many favs to win at all, making it impossible to isolate allergy effects.
   The weight threshold in H19 (max_w=221) may still be correct but is not proven by data alone.
6. Arena identity is irrelevant. Chi-squared tests show 0/20 pirates and 1/40 foods
   with p<0.05 across the 5 arenas (expected ~1 and ~2 by chance). Pirates and foods
   are assigned uniformly — arena names can be ignored.
7. Position within an arena (0-3) causally affects win probability. Evidence chain:
   a. Each pirate appears at each position exactly 25% of the time (±1.5%), with
      sample sizes of ~1200 per position per pirate. This is the simplest indicator
      of random position assignment.
   b. Win rates vary strongly by position: pos0=18.5%, pos1=21.5%, pos2=26.5%, pos3=33.5%.
   c. The key test: for the SAME pirate with the SAME n_fav and n_allergy (controlling
      for all intrinsic factors), average odds vary dramatically by position. Examples
      with 0 fav, 0 allergy (no course-dependent effects at all):
        - Buck Cutlass: pos0=3.68, pos3=2.37
        - Lucky McKyriggan: pos0=6.50, pos3=3.27
        - Squire Venable: pos0=12.76, pos3=9.26
      ~1200 observations per cell. The only variable is position.
   d. Occam's razor: random assignment (explains 25% trivially) + position causes
      win rate changes (explains the above) is far simpler than assuming a weak
      internal ordering that coincidentally produces exactly 25% per pirate.
   e. The same-odds test (50.4% among same-odds pirates in same arena) does NOT
      refute causation — it confirms that the odds-setter correctly accounts for
      the position effect. A weaker pirate at pos3 gets the same odds as a stronger
      pirate at pos0 because position compensates for the strength gap.
   f. Within odds=2, avg strength at pos0=85.9 vs pos3=81.3, confirming that
      intrinsically weaker pirates at advantaged positions achieve the same odds.
   g. The effect is large: approximately ±1.5 odds points, comparable to 1-2 favorites.
      All future hypotheses should include position as an input variable.
