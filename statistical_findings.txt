# Statistical Findings
# Each finding states the result, then how to reproduce it.

## Game inputs

1. **Strength is the primary determinant of base win rate.**
   Reproduce: regress WR on strength at nf=0, na=0.

2. **Favorites provide ~8-10% multiplicative speed boost per fav.**
   Reproduce: compare WR ratios at nf=0,1,2,3 for same pirate.

3. **Allergies reduce win rate via weight-dependent damage.**
   Formula: `weight_offset = min(floor((221-w)/2), 7)`, rolled per allergy.
   Reproduce: compare allergy WR penalty for heavy vs light pirates.

4. **Orvinn is NOT immune to allergies (p=0.002).** Our model predicts wo=0
   (weight=221 → floor((221-221)/2)=0), meaning zero allergy damage. But
   regressing excess win rate (actual - model prediction) on na shows a
   significant negative slope: b=-0.018, t=-3.09, p=0.002. Each allergy
   course costs Orvinn ~1.5-1.8pp of win rate that the wo=0 model misses.
   This survives controlling for: opponent total nf (p=0.002), own nf
   (p=0.0008), and opponent fav overlap with Orvinn's allergy courses
   (p=0.002). The opponent-fav confound (Orvinn's allergy courses boosting
   opponents) is real (r=0.66 between Orvinn na and opponent fav overlap)
   but the model already accounts for opponent nf, so it doesn't explain
   the residual. However, forcing wo=7 on Orvinn makes the fit dramatically
   worse (LL drops by 0.35/arena), so the true allergy effect is small,
   somewhere between wo=0 and wo=7.

4b. **The allergy formula over-penalizes Gooblah (p=0.00005).** Gooblah
    (wo=7, str=93) shows a significant POSITIVE slope of excess WR on na:
    b=+0.029, t=4.07, p=0.00005. The model predicts allergies hurt him more
    than they actually do. This survives controlling for opponent nf and own
    nf. Combined with finding 4, this suggests the formula
    `wo = min(floor((221-wt)/2), 7)` may be wrong — too low for Orvinn
    (wt=221) and too high for Gooblah (wt=199).
    Reproduce: run orvinn_allergy_test analysis — Gooblah section.

4c. **Orvinn's allergy effect disappears around round 8616 (Dec 2022).**
    Sliding window analysis (1000-day windows, step=100) shows the na slope is
    significantly negative in early windows (days 0-400: slope=-0.025, p=0.012)
    and becomes flat/positive after day ~3700 (slope=+0.005, p=0.64). Breakpoint
    search with refinement finds the sharpest split at day 4450 in the dataset,
    which corresponds to round 8616 (2022-12-08):
      Before (n=4450): slope=-0.018, t=-3.69, p=0.0002
      After  (n=1193): slope=+0.004, t=+0.44, p=0.66
    WR by na before: 14.2%→11.0%→9.9%→5.7%→0.0% (clear gradient).
    WR by na after:  11.1%→10.6%→11.7%→9.4%→5.6% (flat).
    Overall WR is unchanged (10.83% vs 10.81%, p=0.99) — the effect is too small
    (~0.3pp expected from dice(1,0)=1) to detect in raw WR, only visible in the
    na-gradient pattern. Consistent with a PHP upgrade changing `rand(1,0)` from
    returning 1 (old PHP: min>max returns min) to returning 0 (modern PHP: min>max
    returns false/0). This may explain why test LL > train LL: post-change behavior
    (wo truly =0) matches our wo=0 model better than pre-change (wo effectively =1).
    Dataset spans rounds 4166 (2010-10-01) to 9808 (2026-03-13), 5643 days total.
    Reproduce: run orvinn_temporal_test analysis — sliding window slopes,
    breakpoint search, WR-by-na stratification.

5. **Weight has no effect beyond allergy damage.** Same-strength pirates
   (str=81: Franchisco w=165, Federismo w=166, Tailhook w=207) at na=0 show
   no consistent WR ordering by weight across fav counts 0-4.
   Reproduce: filter str=81 pirates to na=0, compare WR by nf bucket.

6. **Strength and weight are not rounded.** The odds-vs-strength curve is smooth;
   str=79 vs str=81 gives clearly different odds (~5.15 vs ~4.47).
   Reproduce: plot avg odds vs strength at nf=0, na=0.

7. **Overlap foods (both fav AND allergy) count as allergy only** (elseif pattern).
   Overlap WR ratio = 0.77x baseline, matching pure allergy (0.79x), not fav (1.28x).
   Reproduce: compare WR for 6 overlap pirates at (pure_fav, pure_allergy, overlap) conditions.

8. **Courses are fungible.** Only the count of favs/allergies matters, not which
   specific courses or categories. Multi-category courses give no extra boost;
   same-category vs different-category fav pairs are interchangeable.
   Reproduce: compare WR by category source at fixed nf/na; chi-sq within-category.

## Arena structure

9. **Arena identity is irrelevant.** 0/20 pirates and 1/40 foods show p<0.05
   variation across arenas (expected by chance). Identical position gradients
   across all 5 arenas.
   Reproduce: chi-squared per pirate/food across arena names.

10. **Food slot position (0-9) does not affect win rates.** No pirate position x
    food position interaction either.
    Reproduce: compare WR by food slot at each pirate position.

11. **Pirate grouping into arenas is random.** Indistinguishable from random
    partition of 20 into 5x4. Pairwise co-occurrence chi-sq p=0.61; strength
    sum distribution matches simulation; no serial correlation.
    Reproduce: pairwise/triplet co-occurrence tests, strength sum simulation.

12. **Arena outcomes within a day are independent.** Pairwise upset correlations
    all |r|<0.03. Winner-odds variance ratio = 0.992 (z=-0.39).
    Reproduce: correlate upset indicators across arena pairs within days.

13. **Foods assigned independently of pirate identity.** Per-pirate fav counts
    match hypergeometric expectation. Pairwise food co-occurrence explained
    entirely by marginal frequency deviations (24.3-25.5%).
    Reproduce: compare observed fav counts to hypergeometric PMF per pirate.

14. **Pirate scores within an arena are independent.** H2H win rates stable
    across terciles of opponent strength (12/190 pairs p<0.05, expected 9.5).
    Reproduce: compute P(A wins | A or B won) across opponent-strength terciles.

## Algorithm structure

15. **Elimination models underperform score models.** Best elimination LL=-1.110
    vs best score model LL=-1.078. Elimination is too deterministic.
    Reproduce: run food_unified.rs with elimination vs score model configs.

16. **Algorithm unchanged across dataset.** Splitting 4835 days in half: 0/20
    pirates differ at p<0.05. Position gradient stable across halves.
    Reproduce: split data by date, compare per-pirate WR and position gradient.

## Position effect

17. **Position causally affects win probability.** Win rates: P0=18.5%, P1=21.5%,
    P2=26.5%, P3=33.5%. Pirates are randomly assigned (25% each position).
    Same pirate, same nf/na shows dramatically different odds by position
    (e.g. Buck Cutlass: pos0=3.68, pos3=2.37 at nf=0,na=0).
    Reproduce: compute avg odds by position for each pirate at fixed nf/na.

18. **Both die-size modification AND tiebreaking contribute.** Position gradient
    varies from 10.7pp (lopsided arenas) to 18.1pp (balanced arenas). Gooblah
    vs weak opponents: 11.5pp gradient (p=0.0001), where tiebreak-only model
    predicts <5pp. Regression interaction pos x balance: t=8.7, p<0.0001.
    Reproduce: run arena_balance_test.md analysis (Tests A-D).

19. **Position effect is multiplicative on die size**, not additive. Gradient in
    pp scales with strength: 9pp (str<=70) vs 19pp (str 81-90). Multiplicative
    predicts larger pp shift for strong pirates (small die); additive predicts
    the opposite.
    Reproduce: compute P3-P0 gradient stratified by own strength bin.

20. **Position effect is independent of fav count.** Gradient ~13-17pp at every
    fav count 0-5. Adjacent pairs where both have 0 favs still show ~54-61%
    h2h advantage for later position. Rules out $n_fav leaking across the loop.
    Reproduce: matched-fav h2h and own-fav gradient analysis.

21. **Position effect is independent of allergy count.** Matched-allergy h2h
    stable at 53-57% across na=0-3. Gradient stable at ~14-15pp across total
    arena allergy levels. Rules out $na leaking across the loop.
    Reproduce: matched-allergy h2h and allergy gradient analysis.

22. **Position effect does not depend on earlier pirate's strength.** Matched-
    strength h2h stable at ~55-59% across all strength bins.
    Reproduce: matched-strength h2h analysis.

23. **Cannot distinguish flat constant (+7) from weight_offset accumulation.**
    17/20 pirates have weight_offset=7 (hit the cap). The 3 exceptions (Orvinn
    wo=0, Squire wo=4, Stuff-A-Roo wo=5) are the weakest pirates, confounding
    weight with strength.
    Reproduce: weight_offset accumulation analysis.

24. **Position steps are slightly increasing (later steps larger).** Strength-
    matched h2h log odds ratios: step 0→1 = 0.200, 1→2 = 0.224, 2→3 = 0.250.
    Consistent with $adj += 7 multiplicative model (die ratios 93/100, 86/93,
    79/86 are decreasing fractions, so later steps shrink die proportionally
    more). Range is only 1.24pp (~1.4σ), so suggestive but not conclusive.
    Reproduce: strength-matched h2h for adjacent pairs (0,1), (1,2), (2,3).

25. **Position h2h odds ratios compose multiplicatively.** Under strength
    matching, non-adjacent h2h rates predicted from adjacent steps match
    observed within 2% (ratios 0.979–1.011). Confirms position acts as a
    consistent per-position multiplicative factor independent of pirate identity.
    Note: raw (unmatched) composability is a tautology — OR(i,j) cancels by
    construction. Only strength-matched composability is informative.
    Reproduce: compute all 6 pairwise h2h rates, strength-matched; compare
    OR(0,2) vs OR(0,1)*OR(1,2), etc.

## Odds maker

26. **Opening odds = max(2, min(13, floor(1/p))).** The odds maker computes the
    true win probability p (likely via monte carlo), then displays floor(1/p)
    clamped to [2, 13]. Evidence: empirical win rates at each odds level match
    floor-rule midpoints to 3 decimal places for odds 3-5 (obs 0.288/0.225/0.184
    vs predicted 0.292/0.225/0.183). Floor SSE=368 vs round=1052 vs ceil=1952.
    Odds=2 and odds=13 are clamped bins absorbing extra mass (odds=2 WR=0.523
    because all p>0.50 maps to 2; odds=13 WR=0.042 because all p<0.077 maps
    to 13). The 1/WR at each odds level lands just above the odds value (e.g.
    odds=5 -> 1/WR=5.45), consistent with floor rounding.
    Reproduce: compute empirical WR at each opening odds value, compare against
    predicted midpoints for floor/ceil/round rules.

27. **Odds=2 is the only systematically +EV single-arena bet.** Floor rounding
    guarantees EV = p*odds <= 1.0 for odds >= 3 (since floor(1/p)=N implies
    p <= 1/N). But odds=2 is clamped — all p > 1/3 maps to odds=2, including
    p > 0.50 where EV = p*2 > 1.0. Confirmed by backtest over 32,154 bets:
      odds=2:  ROI = +4.7%, 95% CI [+3.6%, +5.8%], z=+8.41 (p<0.0001)
      odds=3:  ROI = -13.7%, CI [-16.1%, -11.3%], z=-11.13 (p<0.0001)
      odds=4:  ROI = -10.1%, CI [-13.5%, -6.7%], z=-5.89 (p<0.0001)
      odds=5:  ROI = -8.2%, CI [-12.3%, -4.2%], z=-3.98 (p=0.0001)
      odds 6-12: CIs all contain zero (insufficient data to determine)
      odds=13: ROI = -45.9%, CI [-49.1%, -42.6%], z=-27.75 (p<0.0001)
    Odds=2 ROI is stable across time (4.1-5.3% at 1000-day checkpoints).
    Reproduce: bet 1 unit on every pirate at each odds level, compute
    cumulative profit and per-bet return variance.

## Betting strategy

28. **The entire betting edge from opening odds comes from the odds=2 clamp.** A
    top-10-EV strategy using ANY opening odds gets +78.5% ROI (+44318 profit).
    Restricting to only 2:1 parlays gets +65.5% ROI (+36891). Excluding ALL 2:1
    pirates gives -0.7% ROI (-411), i.e. break-even. The opening odds maker is
    essentially perfect for odds >= 3. Note: current_odds (updated later in the
    day based on bet volume) may still contain exploitable edge — this analysis
    only covers opening odds.
    Reproduce: run backtest.rs strategies make_bets_top_ev, make_bets_2s_parlays,
    make_bets_no_2s.
    See findings 41-47: this was attacked directly and survived, except that
    the current_odds caveat above turns out to be a real +21.7% edge (47).

29. **Model-filtered odds=2 singles are highly profitable.** Filtering odds=2
    pirates by model win probability dramatically improves ROI:
      no filter:  +4.7% ROI (32154 bets)
      p >= 0.50:  +29.2% ROI (15022 bets)
      p >= 0.55:  +36.0% ROI (11763 bets)
      p >= 0.60:  +42.2% ROI (9062 bets)
    Reproduce: run backtest.rs make_bets_2s_filtered at various thresholds.

30. **Non-2:1 pirates serve as neutral payout multipliers in parlays.** They add
    no edge themselves but create distinct bet combinations, allowing the same
    good 2:1 edge to be reused across multiple bet slots (max 10 per day).
    An "anchored" strategy — every bet must contain at least one good 2:1 pirate
    (model p >= threshold), padded with any other pirates, EV >= 1.0 — confirms:
      Anchor p>=0.45 + any:  +78.7% ROI, +44279 profit (matches original)
      Anchor p>=0.50 + any:  +80.4% ROI, +44626 profit
      Anchor p>=0.52 + any:  +81.4% ROI, +44670 profit
      Anchor p>=0.55 + any:  +83.6% ROI, +44684 profit
    At p>=0.45, profit matches the original strategy (~44.3k), confirming the
    original's edge comes entirely from 2:1 anchors. Tightening the 2:1 filter
    improves ROI (78.7% -> 83.6%) while keeping total profit flat (~44.6k),
    because bad 2:1s are dropped but the remaining good 2:1s still fill 10 slots
    via non-2:1 padding.
    Reproduce: run backtest.rs make_bets_anchored_2s at various thresholds.

31. **Model-filtered betting cannot produce edge at non-2:1 opening odds.** For each
    odds level 2-13, we filtered pirates by model EV threshold (model_p * odds >= T)
    and swept T from 0.80 to 1.50. Results at each odds level's best threshold:
      odds=2:  +61.8% ROI (3274 bets, z=+35.3) — strong edge, as expected
      odds=3:  +3.3% ROI (572 bets, z=+0.56) — not significant
      odds=4:  +12.2% ROI (164 bets, z=+0.87) — not significant
      odds=5:  +15.2% ROI (282 bets, z=+1.21) — not significant
      odds=6:  +3.1% ROI (489 bets, z=+0.30) — not significant
      odds=7:  +11.0% ROI (82 bets, z=+0.39) — not significant
      odds=8:  +30.0% ROI (80 bets, z=+0.90) — not significant
      odds=9:  +9.5% ROI (723 bets, z=+0.87) — not significant
      odds=10: +13.3% ROI (565 bets, z=+0.99) — not significant
      odds=11: +29.2% ROI (1277 bets, z=+2.94) — nominally significant but fails
               Bonferroni correction (84 tests, need z>3.3)
      odds=12: +30.5% ROI (561 bets, z=+1.93) — not significant
      odds=13: +39.0% ROI (290 bets, z=+1.64) — not significant
    High-odds bets require large ROI for significance because variance per bet
    scales as ~odds² (a 11:1 bet swings +10/-1, so var≈11). With 1500 bets at
    odds=11, you need ~17% ROI just to reach z=2. Odds=11 is the only non-2:1
    level showing z>2, and it doesn't survive multiple-testing correction.
    Confirms: the odds maker is well-calibrated for odds >= 3 and our model
    cannot manufacture edge where none structurally exists.
    Reproduce: run food_unified.rs model-filtered edge analysis with EV sweep.
    Re-tested in finding 44 with out-of-fold NN probabilities and a boundary-null
    bootstrap instead of Bonferroni; same conclusion, with the odds=11 hint
    reappearing and then failing every follow-up test (finding 45).

## Tie-break positional advantage theory

32. **Tie-breaking alone reproduces the positional curve to within noise.**
    A model where 4 equal pirates eat a pile of N food by rolling a die each
    round (subtract roll from pile, finish when pile<=0, ties go to later
    position) produces win rates statistically indistinguishable from the
    empirical curve. Best fit at die=17, N=100 (avg ~12 rounds):
      Model:     P0=0.1806  P1=0.2179  P2=0.2674  P3=0.3342
      Empirical: P0=0.1846  P1=0.2152  P2=0.2657  P3=0.3345
    Chi-squared goodness of fit: chi2=3.87, df=2, p=0.28 (cannot reject).
    Die sizes 15-17 all pass chi2 test with all 4 positions inside 95% CIs.
    The step-ratio discrepancy (model [1.00, 1.32, 1.79] vs empirical
    [1.00, 1.65, 2.25]) is NOT significant — empirical step ratios have
    very wide bootstrap CIs (ratio[1]: [1.18, 2.39], ratio[2]: [1.74, 3.05])
    because dividing noisy quantities amplifies uncertainty.
    Scaling N and d proportionally converges to the same curve — the result
    depends only on the ratio N/d (equivalently, average number of rounds).
    Rolling 2 dice per round does not improve the fit; only (1, d2) pairs
    pass chi2, which are equivalent to a shifted single die.
    Reproduce: compute rounds PMF via convolution, exact win probs with
    tie-break, sweep die sizes, bootstrap CIs on empirical win rates.

## Per-pirate allergy effect stability across PHP cutoff

33. **Only Orvinn's allergy effect disappears at the day-4450 cutoff.**
    Per-pirate OLS (won ~ nf + na + pos) run separately on legacy (days
    0-4449) and post (days 4450+) periods for all 20 pirates. The allergy
    coefficient (na) was tested for change via z = (b_post - b_leg) /
    sqrt(SE_leg² + SE_post²). Results:
      Orvinn (wo=0, w=221):    leg=-0.023 (p<0.0001) → post=+0.001 (p=0.94), change z=+2.20 **
      Peg Leg (wo=7, w=202):   leg=-0.038 (p<0.0001) → post=-0.012 (p=0.34), change z=+1.92 (marginal)
      All other wo=7 pirates:  allergy effect rock-solid in both periods (all post p<0.002)
    Orvinn's result is expected (wo=0 + PHP rand(1,0) change). Peg Leg is
    the 2nd-heaviest wo=7 pirate but is only marginally significant and does
    not survive multiple-testing correction (20 tests). No pirate shows a
    significant strengthening of allergy effect after correction.
    Other findings tested across the cutoff that DO NOT change:
      - Positional advantage curve (all position z < 1.1)
      - Strength effect (same slope, same R²)
      - Favorite food effect (same gradient)
      - Per-pirate anomalies (same outliers persist)
      - Odds=2 edge (still +EV, z=+5.15 in post)
      - Arena identity (still no effect)
    Reproduce: per-pirate OLS (won ~ nf + na + pos) on legacy vs post
    splits, z-test on coefficient change, chi-squared on positional curve.

## Odds maker internals

34. **The odds maker is a simulation, not calibrated from outcomes.** The odds
    maker uses the same game algorithm (including the pre-PHP-fix allergy formula
    for Orvinn) to compute win probabilities, then displays floor(1/p) clamped
    to [2,13]. Evidence: after the PHP upgrade (~round 8616, Dec 2022) that fixed
    Orvinn's allergy immunity (finding 4c), the odds maker STILL penalizes Orvinn
    for allergies identically. OLS regression of Orvinn's odds on na (controlling
    for nf, pos, opponent strength) gives:
      Legacy na coefficient: +0.896 (t=+23.9, p<0.0001)
      Modern na coefficient: +0.937 (t=+12.9, p<0.0001)
    If the odds maker were aware of the change, the modern coefficient would be ~0.
    Instead it's unchanged — the odds maker still thinks allergies hurt Orvinn.
    Calibration by na confirms: in modern at na=0, 1/odds predicts 18.2% but actual
    WR is 11.1% (ratio 0.61); at na=2-3 the odds maker now underestimates Orvinn
    (predicted 10.9%/9.4%, actual 11.7%/9.4%) because his WR is flat across na in
    modern while the odds maker still applies the legacy penalty.
    This also explains why models trained on legacy data perform slightly better on
    modern data: the Orvinn PHP bug added noise to legacy outcomes that any wo=0
    model (which is correct for modern) cannot capture. The naive LL gap between
    regimes is +0.031 for Orvinn arenas but only +0.003 for non-Orvinn arenas;
    the overall gap (+0.0085) is not statistically significant (permutation test
    p=0.15) and disappears entirely when Orvinn arenas are excluded (p=0.38).
    Reproduce: OLS (orvinn_odds ~ na + nf + pos + opp_str) on legacy vs post;
    compare predicted (1/odds) vs actual WR by na in each regime.

35. **Pirate grouping into arenas is confirmed random in both regimes.** Re-tested
    after splitting on the PHP cutoff (finding 4c). Post-legacy (n=1193 days):
    pairwise co-occurrence chi2=163.9 (p=0.91), triplet chi2=1139.3 (p=0.49),
    z-score variance=1.025. No strength-based attraction/repulsion: correlation
    between |strength_diff| and co-occurrence rate r=-0.06 (p=0.41); within-arena
    strength variance matches random simulation (z=-0.44, p=0.66). No serial
    correlation (lag-1 autocorrelation=0.001, same-arena-next-day=20.1%≈20%).
    Position within arena is uniform (2/20 pirates p<0.05, expected 1).
    Legacy regime showed marginal z=2.30 (p=0.02) on arena strength balance but
    this does not replicate in post (z=0.94, p=0.35) and is likely noise.
    Reproduce: run regime_analysis.py (pairwise/triplet co-occurrence, strength
    balance simulation, serial correlation).

36. **Food assignment is confirmed random and independent across arenas.** Post-
    legacy: food frequency uniform (chi2=28.5, p=0.89), food-pirate independence
    (0/20 pirates p<0.05), food pairwise co-occurrence (chi2=771.8, p=0.57),
    cross-arena food overlap=2.506 (expected 2.5, t=0.58, p=0.56). Daily food
    appearance distribution matches Binom(5,0.25) (chi2=5.9, p=0.20). Each arena
    draws 10 foods without replacement from 40; arenas are independent.
    Reproduce: run regime_analysis.py sections 5-6.

37. **No "super favorite" or "super allergy" foods exist.** At fixed pirate and
    fixed nf, win rate does not vary by which specific fav foods are present.
    Chi-sq test across foods within each (pirate, nf) bucket: 0/100 significant
    after Bonferroni, 3/100 at p<0.05 (expected 5). Same for allergies at fixed
    na: 0/73 significant after Bonferroni, 1/73 at p<0.05 (expected 3.7).
    Confirms finding 8 (courses are fungible) at the individual food level.
    Reproduce: per-pirate per-food WR at fixed nf/na, chi-sq within bucket.

38. **No food drafting or distribution among pirates.** All pirates eat all 10
    foods. Tested via: (a) food slot x pirate position interaction shows zero
    signal (all chi-sq p>0.36), (b) log-odds of winning is linear in nf with no
    flattening at high nf (R²=0.90), (c) position advantage is identical whether
    or not two pirates share foods — later position h2h WR = 0.581 with zero
    overlap vs 0.585 with overlap (n=84k pairs). Overlap count (0-6 shared foods)
    shows no gradient in position advantage. Tested fav-only overlap, allergy-
    only overlap, and all cross-type overlaps (fav×alg) — none interact with
    position. The position effect is purely intrinsic to the position slot.
    Reproduce: food slot × position chi-sq; log-odds linearity; h2h WR by
    overlap count across all position pairs.

39. **Allergy capping does not improve NN predictions.** Tested capping na at
    1-5 in the 4-feature NN (str, wt, nf, na). Caps ≤3 hurt significantly
    (na cap=1: -0.015 LL). Caps 4-5 are within noise of baseline (+0.0001).
    The NN already handles allergy counts correctly; the worst-predicted arenas
    (high-allergy pirates winning) are genuine upsets, not model errors.
    Reproduce: train NN with capped na, compare test LL on mixed hash split.

40. **NN prediction accuracy does not depend on food overlap between pirates.**
    Arenas where foods are relevant (fav or allergy) to more pirates show weakly
    worse NN log-likelihood (r=-0.033, p=0.0001), but the odds-maker degrades
    identically (r=-0.031, p=0.0002). This is inherent noise from more food
    effects in play, not a model-specific error. Both models handle food overlap
    equally well.
    Reproduce: bin NN and odds-maker LL by total food relevance across pirates.

## Falsification attempt: "no edge unless opening odds are 2:1"

Findings 41-47 are a deliberate attempt to BREAK finding 28/31. Scripts:
edge_train_nn.py, edge_structural.py, edge_strategies.py, edge_deepdive.py,
edge_odds11.py, edge_verdict.py. Result: the claim survives, with one
important exception (finding 47) that lives outside the opening-odds board.

41. **NN retrained with an out-of-fold protocol; prior NN LLs were optimistic.**
    5-fold CV by day, 3-seed probability ensemble, early stopping on a
    validation split carved out of the *training* folds. Out-of-fold LL:
      base (str, wt, nf, na):            -1.07318 all / -1.06357 modern
      + pirate one-hot + regime flag:    -1.07390 all / -1.06728 modern
      + opening-odds features:           -1.07312 all / -1.06637 modern
      odds maker (normalised 1/N):       -1.09495 all / -1.08829 modern
    The 4 raw features remain sufficient: neither pirate identity nor the odds
    themselves add anything (deltas are within seed noise, and negative on
    modern data). The NN beats the odds maker by +0.0218 nats/arena
    [+0.0191, +0.0243], so it does hold information the odds maker lacks —
    which is the necessary condition for edge, and is not sufficient (see 44).
    Note: nn_winrate_table.txt's -1.05821 is NOT comparable — that script
    early-stops on its own test set. Honest OOF cost ~0.015 nats.
    Reproduce: edge_train_nn.py {base,ident,market}; edge_nn_calibration.py.

42. **No Dutch book exists on the opening board, and none can.** Betting
    1/N_i on every pirate in an arena returns exactly 1, so sum_i 1/N_i < 1 is
    risk-free arbitrage. It occurs in 11030/28215 arenas (39.1%) — every single
    one of which contains a 2:1 pirate, where 1/2 is not an upper bound on p
    (the 2 bin is clamped from above). Over the 1067 arenas with no 2:1, the
    minimum is 1.0095 and the condition never fires. More generally p_i >=
    1 - sum_{j!=i} u_j, so a bet at N>=3 is *provably* +EV from opening odds
    alone only when the arena's upper bounds sum below 1 — which requires no
    2:1 pirate. The 2:1 clamp is therefore not merely the largest edge on the
    board, it is the only one obtainable without a model.
    The mirror-image check also passes: sum of bin lower bounds never exceeds
    1 (max 0.9762), so the floor rule is internally consistent everywhere.
    Reproduce: edge_structural.py TEST 2.

43. **~800 outcome-blind cells scanned for a WR > 1/N violation; none found.**
    Under floor rounding WR > 1/N is impossible for N>=3, so every cell is a
    falsification chance. Multiplicity handled by a max-z bootstrap under the
    *boundary* null p_i = 1/N_i (EV exactly 1.0 — the friendliest reading of
    H0; independent simulation over-states variance, so p-values are
    conservative).
      11 odds bins:                    max z=1.46 (odds=11), FWER p=0.56
      160 (pirate x odds) cells:       max z=2.88, FWER p=0.36
      162 (pirate x nf/na) cells:      max z=1.60, FWER p=0.999
      210 (odds x pos/nf/na/regime/overround) cells: max z=1.90, FWER p=0.99
    Bins 9, 10 and 11 sit above their ceilings (+2.2%, +3.4%, +8.3% ROI) but
    none individually or jointly reaches significance (pooled 9-12: z=1.05).
    Targeted priors also fail: Orvinn modern na>=1 at odds>=3 is -22.5% ROI
    despite finding 34's stale-odds-maker result, and Gooblah na>=2 is +10.6%
    (z=1.08). Split-half cell replication is flat (+10.7% and +1.2%).
    Reproduce: edge_structural.py TESTS 1, 3, 3b, 4, 5, 6.

44. **The NN cannot manufacture edge: realised WR tracks min(p_nn, 1/N).**
    The decisive test. Two accounts make different numeric predictions on the
    same bets — M1 "the NN is right" (delivered ROI = claimed) versus M0/H0
    "the ceiling binds" (delivered = capped at 1/N, i.e. 0.0%):
      T      bets   claimed   capped   delivered   z vs M1   z vs M0
      1.00  13936     +9.1%    +0.0%       -0.1%     -4.35     -0.02
      1.05   8439    +13.5%    +0.0%       +4.1%     -3.30     +1.45
      1.10   4769    +18.2%    +0.0%       +8.3%     -2.52     +2.10
      1.20   1423    +27.7%    +0.0%       +3.2%     -3.28     +0.42
      1.30    409    +37.0%    +0.0%       -9.8%     -3.46     -0.72
    Across every threshold the NN's claimed edge is rejected (up to 4.4 sigma)
    while H0's capped prediction fits. The NN's within-bin ranking IS real
    (Stouffer trend z=8.84 across bins) — it just never survives contact with
    the ceiling, which is exactly what floor rounding predicts. Bets where
    only one of the three NN variants claims EV>=1.15 return -0.2%.
    Parlays behave as H0 requires too: forcing the daily top-10 EV parlays to
    contain no 2:1 pirate returns -8.0% over 56430 bets (z=-3.18), and the
    4268 bets in arenas with no 2:1 pirate at all return -7.9% [-9.8%, -5.9%],
    comfortably inside the [1-N(S-1), 1] window H0 allows.
    Reproduce: edge_verdict.py section 1; edge_strategies.py sections 2-8.

45. **The single surviving anomaly (odds=11 + NN EV>=1.10) is best read as
    noise.** 353 bets, WR 14.45% vs a 9.09% ceiling, +58.9% ROI, z=+3.50.
    It fails every follow-up:
      - Multiplicity: FWER p=0.067 within its own (odds,T) family alone, before
        counting the other ~750 cells searched.
      - No neighbours: the identical NN cut gives +8.5% at odds=10 and -10.1%
        at odds=12. A real mispricing does not respect a bin boundary.
      - No mechanism: 352 distinct arenas over 345 days, spread across pirates,
        positions, nf and na; odds internally consistent (rank inversions vs
        the NN are *lower* than average, 0.674 vs 0.771); manual inspection of
        the top arenas shows nothing unusual.
      - It beats its own discoverer: the NN predicted p=0.1093, reality gave
        0.1445 — a +2.12 sigma overshoot of the model that selected it. A
        detected mispricing should land on the model's number, not above it.
      - Strict walk-forward: train-on-first-half gives +10.5% (z=0.82) out of
        sample; train-on-first-2/3 gives -0.8% (z=-0.05).
      - Remove odds=11 and the headline "EV>=1.15, odds>=3" strategy falls from
        +14.2% (z=2.75) to +8.1% (z=1.52). Dropping any other bin changes
        nothing. The entire NN result rests on this one pocket.
      - The implied error is implausibly large: taking it at face value, the
        odds maker should have priced these pirates at 6.9:1 rather than 11:1,
        a four-level miss by a simulation that is otherwise accurate to the
        third decimal place (finding 26).
    Reproduce: edge_odds11.py; edge_deepdive.py; edge_verdict.py section 2.

46. **Power: what a null result rules out.** One-sided 95% upper confidence
    limits on true ROI per opening odds level: N=3 -11.7%, N=4 -7.3%,
    N=5 -4.8%, N=6 +2.6%, N=7 +0.3%, N=8 +6.4%, N=9 +11.0%, N=10 +12.4%,
    N=11 +17.9%, N=12 +7.0%, N=13 -43.2%; odds 3-12 pooled -4.6%. So a
    uniform edge above ~5% at odds 3-5 or above ~3% pooled is excluded, but
    high-odds bins remain individually weak: variance scales as odds^2, so
    odds=11 needs ~17% ROI over 3000 bets just to reach z=2. Confirming the
    odds=11 pocket at z=3.5 needs ~528 bets at its observed (inflated) effect
    size — 23 years of play, since it fires 22.8 times a year — and ~3600 bets
    (~158 years) if the true edge is the +20% the NN actually claims. Sample
    size, not model quality, is the binding constraint on this question.
    Reproduce: edge_structural.py TEST 9.

47. **The claim is false for payout drift: opening odds >= 3 whose CURRENT odds
    have risen are structurally +EV.** This follows from H0 itself. Floor
    rounding means opening odds N implies p > 1/(N+1) for N in 3..12, so any
    payout of N+1 or better has EV > 1 with no model at all. Backtest paying
    at current odds:
      open 3-12, current >= open+1: 20073 bets, +21.7% ROI [+17.9%, +25.6%]
      open 3-12, current >= open+2: 11953 bets, +30.9% ROI [+25.3%, +36.4%]
      open 3-12, current == open:   19707 bets,  -7.3% [-9.8%, -5.1%]  (control)
      open 3-12, current <  open:    2945 bets, -30.4% [-35.8%, -24.7%] (control)
    (95% CIs are day-clustered bootstraps.)
    The guaranteed floor for the first row is EV=1.097, and it delivers more.
    This does NOT contradict findings 27/31: the same bets pay -4.8% at their
    opening odds, and their win rate stays below the opening upper bound
    (z=-4.15 against mean 1/N). The opening odds are not wrong — they are a
    *certificate* that p exceeds 1/(N+1), and drift makes that certificate
    cashable. Odds=13 is excluded: being clamped from below it certifies
    nothing (current==13 pays -47.9%).
    Caveat: assumes the recorded current_odds could be locked in by betting
    late in the day, which is how Food Club odds work but is not verified from
    this data alone.
    Reproduce: edge_structural.py TEST 8; edge_strategies.py section 9.
