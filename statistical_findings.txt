# Statistical Findings

## Constraints for all future hypotheses
1. Strength must be the primary determinant of base win rate
2. Favorites must provide a multiplicative speed boost (~8-10% per fav)
3. Allergies must reduce win rate via a weight-dependent mechanism
4. Heavier pirates must be more resistant to allergy effects
5. Orvinn's apparent allergy immunity is inconclusive — his low strength (52) means
   he needs many favs to win at all, making it impossible to isolate allergy effects.
   The weight threshold in H19 (max_w=221) may still be correct but is not proven by data alone.
6. Arena identity is irrelevant. Chi-squared tests show 0/20 pirates and 1/40 foods
   with p<0.05 across the 5 arenas (expected ~1 and ~2 by chance). Pirates and foods
   are assigned uniformly — arena names can be ignored.
7. Position within an arena (0-3) causally affects win probability. Evidence chain:
   a. Each pirate appears at each position exactly 25% of the time (±1.5%), with
      sample sizes of ~1200 per position per pirate. This is the simplest indicator
      of random position assignment.
   b. Win rates vary strongly by position: pos0=18.5%, pos1=21.5%, pos2=26.5%, pos3=33.5%.
   c. The key test: for the SAME pirate with the SAME n_fav and n_allergy (controlling
      for all intrinsic factors), average odds vary dramatically by position. Examples
      with 0 fav, 0 allergy (no course-dependent effects at all):
        - Buck Cutlass: pos0=3.68, pos3=2.37
        - Lucky McKyriggan: pos0=6.50, pos3=3.27
        - Squire Venable: pos0=12.76, pos3=9.26
      ~1200 observations per cell. The only variable is position.
   d. Occam's razor: random assignment (explains 25% trivially) + position causes
      win rate changes (explains the above) is far simpler than assuming a weak
      internal ordering that coincidentally produces exactly 25% per pirate.
   e. The same-odds test (50.4% among same-odds pirates in same arena) does NOT
      refute causation — it confirms that the odds-setter correctly accounts for
      the position effect. A weaker pirate at pos3 gets the same odds as a stronger
      pirate at pos0 because position compensates for the strength gap.
   f. Within odds=2, avg strength at pos0=85.9 vs pos3=81.3, confirming that
      intrinsically weaker pirates at advantaged positions achieve the same odds.
   g. The effect is large: approximately ±1.5 odds points, comparable to 1-2 favorites.
      All future hypotheses should include position as an input variable.
8. Food position (slot 0-9) within an arena does NOT affect win rates.
9. No pirate position × food position interaction. A pirate at pos 0 with a fav
   at food slot 0 wins at the same rate as with a fav at food slot 9 (~0.21).
   Same for pos 3 (~0.36). The position mechanism and food mechanism are independent.
10. All 5 arenas (Harpoon Harry's, Hidden Cove, Lagoon, Shipwreck, Treasure Island)
    show identical position gradients: P0≈18.5%, P1≈21.5%, P2≈26.5%, P3≈33.5%.
    No arena-specific position effects.
11. Phase-based elimination models (3-phase allergy→base→fav, 3-round combat,
    allergy pre-elimination) all underperform the 3-roll score model (PosMul).
    Best elimination: H4PhaseB LL=-1.110 vs PosMul LL=-1.084.
    Elimination makes outcomes too deterministic — strong pirates always outlast
    weak ones, reducing upset rates below historical levels.
12. When a food belongs to multiple categories and triggers both a pirate's favorite
    AND allergy, the game treats it as ALLERGY ONLY (the favorite is negated).
    Six pirates have overlap foods (e.g. Lucky McKyriggan: "Fungi Pizza" is both
    Gross foods [fav] and Pizza [allergy]). 36.7% of matches contain at least one
    overlap. Per-pirate strength-controlled test (N≥100 per cell):
      Pure fav (1,0,0):     WR ratio = 1.28× baseline (helps)
      Pure allergy (0,1,0): WR ratio = 0.79× baseline (hurts)
      Overlap (0,0,1):      WR ratio = 0.77× baseline (hurts, ≈ allergy)
    The overlap ratio matches pure allergy almost exactly (0.77 vs 0.79) and is
    nowhere near fav (1.28) or neutral (1.0). Model-independent result.
13. Weight has NO effect beyond determining allergy damage. Tested using the three
    str=81 pirates (Franchisco w=165, Federismo w=166, Tailhook Kid w=207) filtered
    to matches with 0 allergies, where the allergy formula is inert. Results:
    a. At every fav count (0-4, N=50-500+ per bucket), win rates across the three
       pirates show 2-8pp spreads with no consistent ordering by weight. The heaviest
       (w=207) is sometimes highest, sometimes lowest.
    b. Same-strength pairs elsewhere confirm: Bonnie (w=116, str=76) beats Blackbeard
       (w=171, str=76) at fav=2 (WR 0.287 vs 0.233); Sproggie (w=112, str=73) beats
       Peg Leg (w=202, str=73) (WR 0.253 vs 0.198). Lighter pirates win more often.
    c. Regressing all-pirate WR on strength at fav=1,2 (allergy=0) and correlating
       weight with the residual gives r=0.4-0.5, but with only 17-19 data points
       this is not significant and is contradicted by the same-strength pairs above.
    d. Head-to-head tests (str=81 pairs in same arena, same fav count, both allergy=0)
       yield N=3-9 decisive outcomes per pair -- too small, results straddle 50%.
    Conclusion: weight enters the model solely through the allergy damage formula
    (weight_offset = min(floor((221-w)/2), max_effect)). No speed bonus, no fav
    scaling, no hidden per-weight attribute.
14. Strength and weight are NOT rounded to nearest 5, 10, or any other step size.
    a. The odds-vs-strength curve at (nf=0, na=0) is smooth and continuous with no
       flat plateaus or jumps at 5/10 boundaries. Every strength point produces a
       measurably different average odds value.
    b. Pirates at str=79 and str=81 get clearly different odds (~5.15 vs ~4.47, a
       0.7-point gap), ruling out round-to-nearest-5 (both would map to 80).
    c. Franchisco (s=81, w=165) vs Federismo (s=81, w=166) have near-identical odds
       distributions (e.g. at nf=1,na=1: odds=2 at 32.6% vs 33.6%), consistent with
       raw 1-point resolution. The small residual differences track opponent composition.
    d. Same-strength pirates show 0.5-0.8 odds point variation at the same (nf, na)
       even at (0,0) where weight is irrelevant. These differences are entirely from
       the opponent composition effect (established by the odds maker analysis showing
       r=0.49 correlation between odds and avg opponent strength). This opponent noise
       would mask rounding effects smaller than ~3 strength points, but the smooth
       overall curve and the str=79 vs str=81 gap rule out all plausible step sizes.
    e. Weight rounding is also ruled out: the allergy damage formula already uses
       integer division (floor((221-w)/2)) and caps at max_effect, so many pirates
       share the same weight_offset regardless of exact weight. No additional
       quantization is needed or observed.
15. Pirate scores within an arena are independent. For every pair of pirates
    (190 pairs, N=60-665 h2h outcomes each), the head-to-head win rate
    P(A wins | A or B won) was computed across terciles of the other two
    pirates' combined strength (weak/mid/strong opponents).
    a. Chi-squared test: 12/190 pairs (6.3%) show p<0.05 variation across
       terciles, vs 9.5 (5.0%) expected by chance. No evidence of dependence.
    b. The top-spread pairs show the stronger pirate's h2h rate increasing
       against strong opponents (e.g. Buck vs Ned: 58.4% -> 80.7%). This is
       predicted by the independent-score model: when opponents are strong,
       P(A wins)/P(B wins) amplifies because P(A > strong_opp)/P(B > strong_opp)
       grows when A is stronger than B. The observed spreads (mean 0.069,
       median 0.060) are consistent with this statistical artifact.
    c. No pair shows a pattern inconsistent with independence. The model
       assumption that each pirate generates a score independently, then the
       highest score wins, is fully supported by the data.
16. Courses are fungible. Only the COUNT of favorites/allergies matters, not the
    identity of specific food courses or which categories they belong to. Tested
    via six independent analyses:
    a. Multi-category courses provide no extra boost. Three pirates (Scurvy Dan,
       Franchisco, Federismo) have courses matching 2+ favorite categories
       (e.g. Hotfish = Salty foods + Meats for Scurvy Dan). In ~40 controlled
       comparisons holding nf_binary and na_binary constant while varying the
       number of double-category courses, only 1/40 reaches p<0.05, consistent
       with chance. No consistent directional effect. The game counts per-course,
       not per-category-match.
    b. Within-category interchangeability confirmed. All 8 Meats-category
       courses produce statistically identical win rates when they are the sole
       favorite (chi2=5.5, df=7, p>0.5). "Hotfish" (also Salty foods) works
       identically to "Joint Of Ham" (pure Meats).
    c. Binary vs weighted counting gives negligible log-likelihood difference.
       Counting each course as 0-or-1-favorite (binary, N=14505) vs counting
       total category matches per course (weighted) yields delta-LL=4.59,
       or ~0.0003 nats per match. Trivially small.
    d. The odds-setter assigns identical odds regardless of which category the
       single favorite comes from (Welch's t-test: all p>0.5 for Blackbeard,
       Crossblades, and Fairfax). The server does not distinguish categories.
    e. Apparent category-level effects are confounded by allergy-overlap pool
       sizes. Admiral Blackbeard (favs=[Vegetables, Fruits], allergy=[Dairy])
       shows Fruits WR=0.325 vs Vegetables WR=0.123 at nf=1/na=0 (p=0.002).
       But Fairfax (same favs, allergy=[Salty foods]) shows the OPPOSITE
       direction: Fruits WR=0.097 vs Vegetables WR=0.188. The reversal rules
       out an intrinsic category effect. The confound: Blackbeard's Dairy
       allergy reclassifies 3 of 5 Fruit courses as allergies via the overlap
       rule, leaving only 2 pure-Fruit-fav courses vs 5 pure-Veg-fav courses.
       Conditioning on "exactly 1 Fruit fav" creates a highly constrained arena
       composition that systematically affects opponent profiles.
    f. Captain Crossblades' Pizza-vs-Slushies gap (p=0.024) is partially
       explained by a position imbalance (Pizza avg_pos=1.37 vs Slushies
       avg_pos=1.51).
    g. Same-category vs different-category favorites are interchangeable.
       For all 11 multi-category-fav pirates at nf=2/na=0, compared WR when
       both favs came from the same category vs one from each category. Per-
       pirate: 0/10 reach p<0.05, direction inconsistent (5 favor diff, 4
       favor same). Best data: Scurvy Dan (N=73 same, N=233 diff) shows
       WR=0.589 vs 0.588 (p=0.987). Bonnie (N=195 vs 269): 0.287 vs 0.286
       (p=0.983). Raw aggregate z=2.19 (p=0.028) favoring diff-category, but
       this reverses under strength control: normalizing each pirate's WR to
       their nf=0/na=0 baseline gives same-cat ratio=1.39 vs diff-cat
       ratio=1.30 (paired t=-1.22, p>0.2). The raw aggregate was confounded
       by weaker pirates disproportionately appearing in the diff-category
       bucket. The game does not track which category a favorite comes from.
    Conclusion: the model's HashSet<usize> representation of favorite_courses
    (binary per-course, ignoring category multiplicity) is correct.
17. The game algorithm has not changed across the dataset. Splitting 4835 days
    into two halves and comparing per-pirate win rates: 0/20 pirates show a
    significant difference at p<0.05 (expected by chance: 1). Largest shift is
    Lucky McKyriggan at 2.1pp (p=0.106). Position gradient is also stable:
    first half 18.7%/21.7%/26.8%/32.8% vs second half 18.3%/21.2%/26.2%/34.3%.
    All model parameters can be fit to the full dataset without concern for
    regime changes.
18. Pirate grouping into arenas is random. Six independent tests confirm:
    a. Within-arena strength variance is 0.6% lower than random expectation
       (z=-2.57, p=0.01) — statistically significant but practically negligible.
    b. Pairwise co-occurrence: chi-squared p=0.61. 11/190 pairs significant at
       p<0.05 (expected 9.5). Strong+strong ratio=1.004, weak+weak=1.012,
       strong+weak=0.994 — no balancing pattern.
    c. Arena strength sum distribution matches simulation perfectly (std ratio
       1.01). Percentiles align at all levels (P1 through P99).
    d. Conditional opponent strength: correlation r=+0.59 (positive, opposite of
       balancing). Strong pirates do NOT face weaker opponents.
    e. No serial correlation: P(same arena as yesterday)=0.1999 vs expected 0.200.
       P(same group as yesterday)=0.1572 vs expected 0.1579.
    f. Max-min strength spread per arena matches simulation (ratio 0.998, p=0.10).
    g. Triplet co-occurrence: 64/1140 significant at p<0.05 (expected 57).
       Chi-squared p=0.95.
    h. No sorting by strength within arenas (F=0.80).
    i. Per-pirate arena distribution: 3/20 significant (expected 1). Ned the
       Skipper shows non-uniform arena assignment (p=0.003), possibly a minor RNG
       bias in arena-name mapping, but does not affect grouping mechanics.
    The assignment is indistinguishable from a random partition of 20 into 5x4.
19. Arena outcomes within a day are independent. Pairwise upset correlations
    (winner odds>=5): 1/10 pairs reaches p<0.05, all |r|<0.03. Winner-odds
    variance ratio across days = 0.992 (z=-0.39, p=0.70). Two pairs show weak
    negative correlation on winner strength rank (r~-0.03), explained by the
    partition constraint (if a strong pirate is in arena A, they cannot be in
    arena B). No RNG correlation.
20. Food courses are assigned to arenas independently of pirate identity.
    Each arena gets 10 of 40 courses; under random assignment each food has a
    25% appearance rate regardless of which pirates are present. Confirmed by:
    a. Per-pirate mean fav count matches hypergeometric expectation exactly.
       2/20 pirates significant at p<0.05 (expected 1), both showing slightly
       FEWER favs (Ol' Stripey z=-2.89, Puffo z=-2.02). 0/20 for allergies.
    b. Distribution of nf counts across 8 different K values (3-13 fav courses)
       matches the hypergeometric PMF at every level.
    c. Global food frequency: 3/40 foods significant (expected 2). Chi-squared
       p=0.79. Each food appears in ~25% of arenas.
    d. Aggregate across all 164 (pirate, fav-food) pairs: observed/expected
       ratio = 0.997 (z=-1.66, p=0.097). For allergies: 0.999 (z=-0.35, p=0.73).
    e. Mean total favs per arena (sum of 4 pirates) = 8.17 vs expected 8.20.
    The game does not boost or suppress a pirate's favs/allergies in their arena.
    f. Pairwise food co-occurrence: 79/780 pairs significant at p<0.05 (expected
       39) with mean z^2=1.41, but this is entirely explained by per-food marginal
       frequency deviations from 25% (range 24.3%-25.5%). After controlling for
       marginals: 33/780 significant (expected 39), mean z^2=0.89. No residual
       pairwise structure. Each arena draws 10 foods independently from 40.
